Reading the evidence

Each target opens with a current interpretation and its main uncertainty. Studies are ordered by clinical relevance and design, not simply by date. Related analyses and follow-ups are grouped with known cohort overlap explained. Historical observations and research background remain optional; source filters, searches and shared finding links still reach every record.

Reading the atlas

Choose a clinical focus and target, then select a finding in the Evidence panel. The selected study and finding type appear above the brain. Checked tracts and cortical regions provide independent anatomical context; their colors do not show treatment benefit.

Use Show labels for floating names, or hover to reveal one name and its explanation. Click a structure to keep its label and highlight, even with Show labels off; its explanation stays below the brain until you clear the selection. Small brown dots anchor anatomy labels; colored study markers have distinct roles identified in the legend. Reset camera returns to the oblique view while keeping your selections. Reset exploration restores the current target’s suggested view and clears study, finding, search and additional region selections. Targets, Evidence and Layers share one inspector beside the brain. Search atlas also finds lessons and functional background.

Color and interaction

Green structures are background anatomy and do not respond to hover or clicks. Selected target and finding anatomy, colored region layers, tracts and study coordinates remain interactive. Blue related anatomy is shown when a finding does not specify a side; it does not imply a bilateral effect. Colors identify what is shown, not treatment efficacy.

Circuit research · v0.17.0

Johnson 2026 and Ji 2026 add ten findings and two guided lessons under Mechanism & network research. Animal experiments, exploratory human associations and retrospective network validation are labeled separately. Whole-region surfaces provide orientation; they do not reproduce either study’s network, a stimulation field or a validated clinical target. Johnson’s connectivity change did not survive correction, and Ji did not test DTN-guided treatment in a randomized trial. Source review and limitations

Functional background: Dixon 2017

Dixon’s integrative review adds proposed functional roles for sgACC, pgACC, medial and lateral OFC, and lateral PFC. These overlapping roles have different levels of support and do not establish treatment mechanisms. In particular, the sgACC account has limited human causal evidence and the medial OFC account is a hypothesis. The broad rostral ACC parcel does not define pgACC; dACC/aMCC and vmPFC labels depend on each study’s definition.

Compare regional functions in Learn · Dixon 2017: publication. This background review adds no clinical outcome records or spatial geometry.

Reading the anatomy

Tract names follow the source atlas, including the anterior and superior corticostriatal tracts and the frontoparietal segment of the cingulum. General tract lines provide reference anatomy. The separately labeled Li fibers show a released response model; no line represents an individual patient’s treatment pathway. Source tract terminology

sgACC means subgenual anterior cingulate cortex. It is gray matter, where neuronal cell bodies are concentrated. SCC DBS targets nearby white matter, which contains connecting fibers. The pink cortical region helps locate this area but does not mark the exact DBS stimulation site.

Controlled depression DBS trials

Holtzheimer 2017 (SCC) and Dougherty 2015 (VC/VS) add two controlled outcome cards. Neither blinded primary comparison demonstrated a significant active-versus-sham benefit. These additions were checked against published abstracts; full text was not independently reviewed. Counts of findings are not counts of independent studies.

Three spatial layers

Atlas geometry describes reference anatomy. Public functional maps add cortical projections of released group volumes, with separate color meanings. Study evidence describes what a publication measured or proposed. Most fibers are reference anatomy; Li’s released response fibers are explicitly identified, and functional connectivity is not drawn as an anatomical fiber.

Spatial limits

Regional cortical parcels are context for broadly named TMS regions, not individual coil locations or simulated electric fields. Whole-structure masks are not substituted for anteromedial STN, lateral habenula, ventral ALIC, or other smaller clinical subdivisions. Those distinctions are stated in each target view.
